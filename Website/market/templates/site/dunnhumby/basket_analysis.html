What the four columns mean
Avg probability

The average household's chance of returning. A department at 62% does not mean 62% of its shoppers come back.

Expected households

The probabilities added together, so the number of households the model expects back. Unbiased even when many sit near the middle.

High likelihood

A headcount of those at 50% or above. A department full of 45%s expects returners but counts none here, which is why the two differ.

Scored

Households eligible for that department, meaning they have bought from it before. This is the denominator for the other three.

Model Benchmarks & Validation
Chronological holdout testing for the Product Revenue Time Series models — this is model evidence, not a live forecast.
How this comparison works
1. Sliding windows
Each sample contains the selected number of earlier 30-day Product ID revenue periods. Windows overlap when the step is smaller than the lookback.
2. Recursive RNN
Each predicted period is fed into the next. Joint multi-step loss is propagated backward through the forecast sequence.
3. Independent model
The gradient-boosting ensemble predicts each lead from the same observed window without using earlier predictions.
4. Honest comparison
Both models use the same final chronological holdout and are compared on revenue error and Top-5/10/20 ranking quality.
